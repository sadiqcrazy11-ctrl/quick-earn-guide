Best Products to Sell Online: What Actually Sells (Prices, Costs & Free Tools)

By Sadiq Khan · Published October 2026 · 7 min read

Share:WhatsAppTelegramX

Quick Answer: The best products to sell online — the cheapest real ones — are digital: planners and printables on Etsy (start: a $0.20 listing fee), templates on Gumroad (start: $0), ebooks on Amazon KDP (start: $0), and print-on-demand shirts and mugs (start: $0 — you keep a $3–$10 margin per sale). Set honest expectations: most new sellers earn $0–$50/month in their first months, and the free tools listed below are enough to start. Pay for tools only after sales cover them.

Products beat services in one way: you make them once and they can sell while you sleep. They lose in another: nobody has to buy. These are the six product types beginners actually sell — what each one is, what it costs to start, what buyers pay, and what sellers honestly keep.

The screenshots lie by omission: For every seller posting a $10,000 month, thousands of shops with similar products made $0 — millions of sellers compete for the same Etsy and Google searches. Most new shops earn $0–$50/month in their first months, and many never make a first sale at all. The sellers who last treat it as a slow catalog business: 20–50 useful listings, clear photos, one specific niche. Read the numbers below as the normal case, not the highlight reel.

Best Products to Sell Online: Six That Actually Sell

Planners & printables (Etsy)

PDF planners, calendars, habit trackers, and printable wall art. Make it once in free Canva, sell the same file forever. The catch: thousands of near-identical listings — a specific buyer (teachers, new parents, wedding planners) beats a generic one every time.

Start: $0–$4Price: $5–$30Etsy · Gumroad

Templates: Notion, Canva & spreadsheets

Resume templates, social-media kits, budget spreadsheets, Notion dashboards. Buyers pay to skip the setup work. Sell on Etsy or Gumroad, and bundle several templates together to raise the price buyers accept.

Start: $0Price: $9–$49Gumroad · Etsy

Ebooks & short guides (Amazon KDP)

A 30–60 page how-to on one narrow problem. Publishing on KDP is free; the royalty is 70% on ebooks priced $2.99–$9.99 and 35% outside that band. Most self-published ebooks sell a handful of copies — the income comes from a catalog, not one book.

Start: $0Price: $2.99–$19.99Amazon KDP · Gumroad

AI art prints & clipart (Etsy)

AI-generated wall art, clipart packs, and seamless patterns sold as instant downloads. Etsy's rule: you must disclose in the listing that the design was AI-created. This niche saturated fast — specific subjects (nursery art, one dog breed, one hobby) outsell generic sunsets.

Start: $0–$10Price: $8–$40Etsy

Prompt packs & AI resource bundles

Curated prompt collections for ChatGPT or image tools, sold as a PDF or Notion file. Cheap to make and hard to defend — anyone can copy a prompt, so the real value is curation, worked examples, and updates you keep adding.

Start: $0Price: $7–$39Gumroad

Print-on-demand (Printful, Redbubble)

Your design on tees, mugs, and posters that a partner prints and ships only when someone orders. Zero inventory: you keep the gap between the base cost and your price, usually $3–$10 per item. Many designs, one niche audience — that is the whole game.

Start: $0Margin: $3–$10/itemPrintful · Redbubble · Printify

Discounts & free tools: start for $0

Every tool a beginner product seller actually needs has a free tier that is enough to launch. These are the real ones — no affiliate links in this section; this site earns nothing if you sign up.

  • Canva Free. Design planners, ebook covers, and product mockups at $0 — the free tier is enough for your first products. Canva Pro (about $15/month) adds premium elements and resizing you don't need on day one.
  • ChatGPT & Claude free tiers. Draft ebook chapters, listing titles, and product descriptions for $0. Edit everything before it ships — buyers can spot raw AI output, and marketplaces penalize listings that read like it.
  • Google Docs, Sheets & Drive (free account). Write the ebook, build the spreadsheet template, track sales — free with 15 GB of storage. Paid Google Workspace plans offer a 14-day free trial if you later want a business email; you don't need it to start.
  • Gumroad. No monthly fee and no listing fee — you pay 10% + $0.50 only when a sale happens. The right price for a store that hasn't sold anything yet: nothing.
  • Amazon KDP. Publishing is free — Amazon takes its royalty share out of sales, never upfront. The same logic applies to Printful and Redbubble accounts: $0 to open, costs come out of each order.
  • CapCut (free). Product teaser videos and listing clips for Etsy and social posts, without an editor subscription. A 20-second video of your planner in use outsells a static cover photo.
  • Student discounts (real — check eligibility). The GitHub Student Developer Pack bundles pro developer tools free with a school email; Canva for Education is free for eligible K-12 teachers and their students; Microsoft 365 Education is free for students and teachers at eligible schools. All three are official programs on the companies' own sites — never pay a third party for 'discount access'.
Honest bottom line: Your first 20 listings can cost under $5 total — a few Etsy listing fees and $0 everywhere else. Spend your first month on products and photos, not subscriptions. Upgrade a tool only when a paying customer makes the free tier feel small, and be suspicious of any 'discount' that asks for your card before you've sold anything.

FAQ

Q: What are the best digital products for a beginner to sell?
A: Templates and planners lead on profit because they cost almost nothing to make — a Canva template, Notion dashboard, or planner priced at $9–$49 costs time, not inventory. The honest asterisk: most individual listings sell nothing. Sellers who earn steadily run 20–50 listings in one clear niche, not one perfect product.

Q: How much does it cost to start selling on Etsy?
A: $0.20 per listing (each lasts 4 months), plus a 6.5% transaction fee and payment processing when something sells. A first batch of 10–20 products designed free in Canva costs $2–$4 in listing fees — under $20 all-in. Anyone selling you a $500 Etsy course before your first listing is the one making the money.

Q: Is print-on-demand really free to start?
A: Yes — Printful, Printify, and Redbubble charge no signup or inventory fees; production and shipping are deducted from each order. The trade-off is margin: on a $25 t-shirt you typically keep $3–$10. It is a volume business — profit comes from many designs each selling a little, not one design selling a lot.
