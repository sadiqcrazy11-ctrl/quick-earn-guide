How to Find Trending Products on Etsy (Before You List Anything)

By Sadiq Khan · Published November 2026 · 5 min read

Share:WhatsAppTelegramX

Quick Answer: How to find trending products on Etsy: do not guess. Start with Etsy's own free search data, check four things — demand, competition, typical price, and a narrow niche angle — and only then list. A broad product with a million listings is not an opportunity; the same product aimed at one specific buyer often is.

Every week new sellers ask the same question: what is selling on Etsy right now? That is the wrong first question. The right one is: what are buyers searching for, how many sellers already serve them, and can you offer a version aimed at one specific person? This guide shows the free way to answer that, before you spend a dollar on a tool.

How to Find Trending Products on Etsy: Start With Etsy's Own Free Data

Etsy now shows sellers its own search data, and it costs nothing to start:

  • Marketplace Insights — built into Shop Manager. It shows what buyers actually type into Etsy, roughly how often, and how many listings compete for it. You get 15 free searches a week (unlimited on Etsy Plus). One caution straight from Etsy: the tool shows what buyers search even if something is not allowed on Etsy, so checking that an idea is legal and policy-compliant is on you.
  • The search bar — type a few letters and the suggestions are real recent searches. They are not necessarily the most frequent terms, but they tell you the exact words buyers use.
  • Trending Now — on Etsy's homepage, ranked by search volume and recent sales. A fast way to see what is hot this week.
  • Your shop Stats and Search Visibility Dashboard — once you list, these show which of your own terms actually bring visits.

The 4 numbers to check

Run every product idea through these four checks before you make anything:

DemandMarketplace Insights, eRank Monthly TrendsSteady or rising over months, not a one-week spike

Free tools when Etsy's own data is not enough

  • eRank free plan — 5 keyword lookups a day, plus Trend Buzz and Monthly Trends. Paid plans start at $5.99 a month, but only pay when a specific question keeps hitting the free limit.
  • EverBee free plan — 10 searches. Its sales numbers are estimates from third-party data, not Etsy's own records. Treat them as a hint for comparing ideas, never as a promise of what you will earn.
  • Merch Titans free tools — keyword research, a tag generator, a fee calculator, and a trademark checker, with no account needed. Run the trademark check before listing anything with a character, brand, or team name.
  • Other trend tools — some sellers also use tools like RankBuddy's Trend Finder. I have not verified its current pricing, so check its own site before signing up for anything.

A worked example: broad vs narrow

Take planners. The broad idea, "budget planner," has huge demand and huge competition — a hard place for a new shop to be seen. The narrow version, "budget planner for freelancers," has smaller demand but far fewer direct competitors, and the buyer feels it was made for them. The same logic works everywhere: "planner for freelance designers," "homeschool mom weekly planner," "meal prep planner for one person." Narrow beats broad for a new seller almost every time.

Now the fee math, because price decides whether a niche is worth it at all. Etsy charges $0.20 per listing, a 6.5% transaction fee, and payment processing of 3% plus $0.25 — roughly $1.10 on a $10 sale before ads. A $3 printable leaves very little room after that, so the typical price in your niche has to cover fees and your time, or the niche fails no matter how trendy it looks.

Traps that waste new sellers' time

  • Chasing a seasonal spike after everyone else listed. Seasonal products sell in short bursts and buyers search weeks early, so list them 6 to 8 weeks before the date, or skip the season.
  • Copying a bestseller. Same photos, same title, same design — Etsy and buyers both punish copies. Make your own version of the idea.
  • Trusting a tool's sales estimate as fact. Estimates compare ideas; they do not forecast your income.
  • Skipping the trademark check. A trending phrase tied to a brand or character can get a listing removed.

The low-risk test

List 3 to 5 items in ONE narrow angle and watch them for 2 to 4 weeks. Judge by favorites, views, and messages — not by hope. If nothing moves, change the angle, not the whole shop. If one item moves, make more of that. That is the whole method: research first, test small, then double down on what buyers actually respond to.

FAQ

Q: Can I find trending Etsy products completely free?
A: Yes. Marketplace Insights (15 searches a week), the Etsy search bar, Trending Now, and eRank's free plan cover the basics. Pay for a tool only when a specific question keeps hitting a free limit.

Q: Is a product with a million listings a bad idea?
A: Not automatically — a million listings proves demand. It is a bad idea only if you list the same generic version. A narrow angle aimed at one buyer is how new shops enter a big category.

Q: Do sales estimates in research tools tell me what I will earn?
A: No. They are estimates from third-party data, not Etsy's own sales records. Use them to compare ideas, not to forecast income.
