30 Side Hustles Most People Have Never Heard Of (With Real Pay and the Rules)

By Sadiq Khan · Published November 21, 2026 · 9 min read · Updated on November 21, 2026

Share:WhatsAppTelegramX

Quick Answer: The most unusual side hustles that actually pay in 2026 include background acting ($231 a day union minimum), standardized patient work ($20-$30 an hour), piano tuning (about $150 per home), professional line standing ($27 an hour), greeting card verse ($300 per poem), and loan signing agent work ($75-$200 per signing). Each has a hidden gate — an agency, a certification, or a state rule — so check the requirement before you start. None of these is a salary: the average side hustler earns about $885 a month and the median is closer to $200.

Every side hustle list repeats the same ten ideas: DoorDash, tutoring, surveys. But there is a whole second list of jobs most people never hear about — background acting, playing a patient for medical students, tuning pianos, even selling your long hair. A Side Hustle Index audit (November 11, 2026) checked 30 of them with real pay and the rules behind each. Several need a permit or certification that is not obvious at first. Get Paid to Show Up Somewhere

Background extra. Background extras work 10-hour days on film and TV sets. SAG-AFTRA's background minimum under the 2026 TV/theatrical agreement is $231 a day; non-union work commonly pays $100 to $200. Productions hire through agencies like Central Casting, mostly in Los Angeles, New York, Georgia, and Louisiana. Four background days at the union rate is $924 — but the work is concentrated in a few cities, days run 10 hours or more, and bookings are irregular. Sign up with an agency this week; first bookings can land within days.

Standardized patient. Standardized patients play sick for medical, nursing, and PA students, who practice taking histories and delivering bad news — then give feedback. Pay runs $20 to $30 an hour, with Columbia listing $30 and a four-hour minimum, and work comes in bursts around the academic calendar. You apply like it is a job, because it is one: schools hire through their simulation centers, and sessions follow the school calendar, so expect busy weeks and quiet ones.

Professional line standing. Line standers hold places for congressional hearings, Supreme Court arguments, sample sales, and product drops. Washington-area services charge clients $30 to $60 an hour, and TaskRabbit line-standing bookings average about $27 an hour. Twenty hours in line over Black Friday and December toy drops works out to about $540. The gate is a TaskRabbit profile with ratings — new profiles get fewer bookings — and the work itself: overnight waits in bad weather come with the job.

Sensory panelist. Sensory panelists taste and smell products for food companies, a few hours a week, at $16 to $23 an hour — Curion's Emeryville site lists $40 — usually on weekday mornings after a training period. The weekday-morning schedule is the real gate: it fits retirees and people with flexible days, not a 9-to-5 worker.

Pet acting. Pet acting pays too: one recent pet food casting offered $500 for a half day and $750 for a full day for a dog and its trainer. Legitimate agencies take a commission instead of charging upfront fees — any agency that asks you to pay to register your pet is a red flag.

Odd Services People Will Pay For

Piano tuning. A standard home piano tuning costs owners $100 to $250, about $150 typical, and takes 60 to 90 minutes. Three tunings a week at $150 is $450 — but learning to tune well takes months of training or an apprenticeship. This is a slow-burn skill, not a this-weekend start. If you already play piano, the training curve is shorter; if you don't, start with a course and expect months before your first paid tuning.

Loan signing agent. Mortgage closings need a notary who knows the paperwork. Loan signing agents typically earn $75-$200 per signing, and the average standard signing pays $100-$125 for 45-60 minutes of work. Five $100 signings a month hits the $500 mark; three a week is $1,200 a month. Timing is good right now: mortgage rates dipped below 6% in late 2026, triggering a refinancing surge. The gate is real, though. You need a state notary commission ($40-$150) plus certification — the National Notary Association's training runs $65-$200 — and some states limit notary fees so much that signing is not profitable. Check your Secretary of State's website before spending anything. One guide estimates a first $100 signing within 30 days and $1,500-$3,000 a month within 90 days — but only where signings are available, and only after the commission and certification are done.

Golf ball diver. Golf ball divers with open-water certification retrieve balls from course ponds and sell them for about 8 to 12 cents each. The catch: courses require retrieval contracts, and taking balls without one is theft. A thousand balls at 10 cents is $100, so this only pays with a contract and volume.

Robotics video. The strangest online one: robotics companies pay a few dollars to about $25 an hour for first-person video of folding laundry or washing dishes, recorded with a phone strapped to your forehead — and many projects pay only for approved footage. Read the terms before you record: some projects pay per approved clip, not per hour.

Process serving. Process servers deliver court papers, and the pay is per delivery — but the rules are state-specific. In California, serving more than 10 papers a year requires county registration, including a $2,000 bond and fingerprinting. Check your state's rule before your first delivery.

Selling Things You Did Not Know Were Worth Money

Long hair. Long hair that has not been dyed or chemically treated sells to wig makers for about $150 to $600. The price depends on length, thickness, and condition — virgin hair earns the most.

Shed antlers. Shed antlers sell to dog chew makers and crafters, but the rules vary by state: West Virginia prohibits shed hunting, Utah requires a free ethics course, and Colorado closes public land west of I-25 from January 1 to April 30. Check the rule before you pick anything up.

Wild mushrooms. Wild mushrooms sell to restaurants, but several states require foragers to be certified before selling, and Oklahoma does not allow wild-foraged mushrooms to be sold to food establishments at all. Certification first, sales second.

Playtesting mobile games. Playtesting mobile games pays $5 to $9 for a 15-minute test on PlaytestCloud, though invitations arrive irregularly. At $5 to $9 per test, this is pocket money, not income — the invitations, not your effort, set the pace.

AI data projects. AI data projects pay for selfies and short videos, with recent listings offering $140 per approved task. The pay is high because the buyer gets rights to use your face, so read the license terms first. A $140 task that licenses your likeness indefinitely is a different deal from a $140 task with a one-year limit.

Greeting card verse. Blue Mountain Arts pays $300 per poem for greeting card rights. The writing has to be sincere and non-rhyming, and the acceptance rate is selective — this is a craft submission, not a gig. One accepted poem is $300; ten rejected ones are $0.

How to Pick One

Pick one hustle, not five. The fastest to start are the show-up jobs — background extra and line standing need an agency or platform profile this week, and first bookings can land within days. Loan signing agent work is the best-paid of the group per hour of actual work, but the notary commission and certification take days to weeks and cost $105 to $350 before the first signing. Piano tuning, shed hunting, and mushroom foraging need months of learning or a certification first.

Run the 30-day test: give your one pick 30 days, track every dollar and every hour, and never pay upfront fees to anyone who promises you work. Legitimate agencies take a commission from work you do; they do not charge you to register.

FAQ

Q: What is the highest-paying unusual side hustle?
A: Background acting: $231 a day under the SAG-AFTRA 2026 minimum, or $100-$200 non-union — but work is concentrated in a few cities and days run 10 hours or more. Greeting card verse pays $300 per accepted poem, and loan signing agents earn $75-$200 per signing.

Q: Do I need a license for these side hustles?
A: Some do, some do not. Process serving needs county registration in California after 10 papers a year, including a $2,000 bond and fingerprinting. Loan signing agents need a state notary commission plus certification. Wild mushroom sellers need certification in several states. Shed hunting is banned in West Virginia. Always check your state's rule before starting.

Q: Can I really make $500 a month from odd side hustles?
A: Yes, with the right hustle: four background extra days at the union rate is $924, 20 line-standing hours at $27 is $540, and five $100 loan signings is $500. But training, school calendars, and irregular invitations slow most of these down. Pick one hustle, give it 30 days, and never pay upfront fees to anyone who promises you work.

Q: How long does it take to become a loan signing agent?
A: A state notary commission takes days to weeks, and certification adds $65-$200. One guide estimates a first $100 signing within 30 days — but only where signings are available, and some states cap notary fees so low the work is not profitable.
