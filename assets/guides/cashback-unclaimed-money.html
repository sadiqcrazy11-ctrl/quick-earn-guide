Cashback and Unclaimed Money: Free Money You Are Probably Owed

By Sadiq Khan · Published November 19, 2026 · 3 min read · Updated on November 19, 2026

Share:WhatsAppTelegramX

Quick Answer: Millions of Americans have unclaimed money — forgotten bank accounts, security deposits, refunds, and old paychecks — sitting with their state, free to claim. Check your state's unclaimed property site and missingmoney.com: it takes five minutes and costs nothing. Cashback is smaller but steady: 1–2% back on spending you were already doing.

Not every way to get money needs work. Some of it is already yours — sitting in a state account because a bank, an employer, or a utility company could not find you. And some of it is a small rebate on money you spend anyway. Neither will make you rich. Both are worth five minutes of your time.

Unclaimed money: what it is

When a company owes you money and cannot reach you — an old bank account, a security deposit, an uncashed paycheck, a refund, an insurance payout, a forgotten safe-deposit box — the money goes to the state after a few years. States hold billions of dollars this way. One in seven Americans has unclaimed property, and the average claim is worth real money — often hundreds of dollars, sometimes thousands. It is your money, held in trust, and claiming it is free.

How to check in five minutes

  1. Search missingmoney.com. It is the official multi-state search run by the states themselves — enter your name and every state you have lived in.
  2. Check your state's own site too. Search "[your state] unclaimed property" and use the official .gov site — some states list more than the national search.
  3. Check USA.gov's unclaimed money page. It links the federal sources too: IRS refunds, VA benefits, and forgotten pension money.
  4. Claim directly. The state will ask for ID and proof of address — a driver's license and an old bill usually do it. Claims take weeks, not days.

The unclaimed-money scams

Searching is free, and claiming is free. Anyone who charges a fee to "find" your money is selling you a search you can do yourself in five minutes. "Finder" companies that take 10–30% of your claim are legal in some states but never necessary. And no state will ever call, text, or email asking you to pay a fee to release your money — that is a scam, full stop.

Cashback: small, steady, real

Cashback apps and cards return 1–2% of what you spend — typically $100–$200 a year for an average household. The honest rules: only buy what you were already buying, or the "savings" cost you money; use a card you pay off in full every month, or interest eats the cashback ten times over; and stack a cashback app on top of a cashback card when both apply to the same purchase. Cashback is a discount, not income — but a discount you already earned is still money.

Bottom Line

Check for unclaimed money today — it takes five minutes, it is free, and one in seven people finds something. Then set up cashback on spending you already do. Neither replaces a side hustle, but both are money you are leaving on the table right now.

FAQ

How do I check if I have unclaimed money?
Search missingmoney.com — the official multi-state search — plus your own state's unclaimed property site and USA.gov's unclaimed money page. Enter your name and every state you have lived in. It is free, and it takes about five minutes.

Is unclaimed money real or a scam?
It is real: states hold billions in forgotten bank accounts, deposits, refunds, and paychecks. The scams are the companies that charge you to find it — searching and claiming through official .gov sites is always free.

How much cashback can I actually earn?
Typically 1–2% of spending, or about $100–$200 a year for an average household. It is a discount, not income — and it only helps if you buy what you were already buying and pay the card off in full every month.
