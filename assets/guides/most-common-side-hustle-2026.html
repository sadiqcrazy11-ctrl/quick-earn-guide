The Most Common Side Hustle in 2026 Is Not What You Think

By Sadiq Khan · Published October 27, 2026 · 5 min read

Share:WhatsAppTelegramX

Quick Answer: The most common side hustle in 2026 is selling goods online. About 44–48% of side hustlers earn money from ecommerce — well ahead of freelance work at 21–23% and delivery work at 19%. Most sell on peer-to-peer marketplaces like Facebook Marketplace and Poshmark (58% of ecommerce hustlers), Amazon (52%) or eBay (38%). The honest earnings picture is modest: 62% earn up to $500 a month after taxes, and only about 20% clear $1,000 a month.

Ask most people what the typical side hustle looks like and they will picture freelance gigs or a delivery app. The 2026 numbers tell a different story. Selling things — things you already own, things you make, things you find second-hand — is now the biggest side hustle of all. It needs no client, no interview and no boss. You list something, someone buys it, you ship it. That simplicity is exactly why it sits at the top, and it is also why the earnings need an honest look before you start.

Where Sellers Actually Sell

Three kinds of platform carry almost all of it. Peer-to-peer marketplaces — Facebook Marketplace, Poshmark and similar apps — are used by 58% of ecommerce hustlers, because listing is free and local buyers can collect in person. Amazon is used by 52%, mostly for its huge buyer traffic, though fees and competition are higher. eBay, used by 38%, remains the workhorse for second-hand and collectible items because its sold-listings history tells you what things really sell for. Many sellers use more than one, which is why the percentages add up to more than 100.

What People Actually Sell

Three categories dominate, and they are closer than you might expect. Handmade and custom items lead at 36% — personalised gifts, crafts and made-to-order pieces. Vintage and second-hand goods follow at 35%, from clothing to furniture to collectibles. Print-on-demand is also at 35%: designs printed on shirts, mugs and posters only after someone orders, so there is no stock to buy up front. Notice what is missing from the top of the list — bulk-bought inventory from wholesalers. Most ordinary sellers start with what they have or what they can make, not with a warehouse.

The Honest Earnings

Here is the part the highlight reels skip. After taxes, 62% of online sellers earn up to $500 a month from their side hustle — useful money, not life-changing money. About 20% earn over $1,000 a month, and that group is usually months or years in, with repeat buyers and a clear niche. Fees, shipping, packaging and returns all come out before profit, so the sale price is never what you keep. If a course or guru promises you thousands in your first month selling online, they are selling the course, not describing the typical result.

How to Start: 4 Steps

  1. Sell what you already own first. Go through your home for unused electronics, clothes, books and furniture. Your first listings cost you nothing, teach you photographing, pricing and shipping, and prove whether you enjoy the work before you spend a dollar on stock.
  2. Price from sold listings, not asking prices. Anyone can ask $200 for a used jacket; what matters is what similar jackets actually sold for. On eBay, filter by “Sold items” and price near the recent average. On Marketplace, check how long similar listings have sat unsold.
  3. Pick one lane. Handmade, vintage or print-on-demand — choose one and learn it properly. One lane means one kind of buyer, one set of fees to understand, and listings that get better every week. Three half-started lanes usually earn less than one focused one.
  4. Reinvest slowly. When sales come in, put a small share back into better photos, packaging or a little more stock — and only buy stock you have already seen sell. Growing from your own sales keeps the risk at zero; borrowing to buy inventory turns a side hustle into a debt.

The Honest Timeline

First sale: for items you already own, listed at a fair price on a busy marketplace, a first sale within days is realistic — sometimes the same weekend. Steady business: expect 1 to 3 months of regular listing before sales feel consistent rather than lucky. That gap is normal. Most people who quit do so in the first month, right before the routine starts working.

FAQ

Q: What is the most common side hustle in 2026?
A: Selling goods online. About 44–48% of side hustlers earn from ecommerce, ahead of freelance work (21–23%) and delivery work (19%). Peer-to-peer marketplaces like Facebook Marketplace and Poshmark are the most-used channel, followed by Amazon and eBay.

Q: How much do online sellers actually make?
A: Less than social media suggests. After taxes, 62% earn up to $500 a month, and only about 20% earn over $1,000 a month. The higher earners are usually established sellers with a niche and repeat buyers, not beginners in month one.

Q: What should a beginner sell first?
A: Things you already own. Unused items around your home cost nothing to list, teach you pricing and shipping, and can sell within days. Once you know the process, pick one lane — handmade and custom goods (36% of sellers), vintage and second-hand (35%), or print-on-demand (35%) — and build there.
