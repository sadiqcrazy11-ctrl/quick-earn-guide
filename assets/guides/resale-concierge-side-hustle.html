Resale Concierge Side Hustle: Sell Other People's Stuff for a Commission (2026)

By Sadiq Khan · Published November 10, 2026 · 5 min read

Share:WhatsAppTelegramX

Quick Answer: A resale concierge side hustle sells other people's stuff for them — you photograph, list, and ship their items on eBay, Poshmark, or Facebook Marketplace, and keep a commission of roughly 25 to 50 percent of each sale. You buy no inventory, so your startup cost is close to zero. Your first client is usually someone you already know with a closet, garage, or storage unit full of things they will never list themselves.

Most side hustles ask you to buy something first: inventory, a course, a tool subscription. A resale concierge skips that entirely. Your clients already own the inventory. They just do not have the time, patience, or know-how to sell it — and that is exactly what they pay you for.

Resale Concierge Side Hustle: How the Money Works

You are a service provider, not a shop owner. A client hands you items, you research the price, photograph, list, answer buyer questions, pack, and ship. When the item sells, you take your cut and pay the owner the rest. The standard splits look like this:

  • Independent concierge: 40 to 50 percent of the sale price is common for a full-service job — you do everything, including shipping. Some sellers use a net split instead: platform fees and shipping come off the top first, then you split what is left.
  • eBay's official consignment partners: 60 percent to the owner on items under $300, rising to 80 percent on items over $5,000 — but that is their program, not yours, and it is aimed at luxury items.
  • Resale platforms like Sella: sellers keep a sliding share — 80 percent of the first $20, then 50 percent of the next band, 25 percent up to $250, and 10 percent above that — minus the platform's service fee.

The honest math: a $100 sale at a 50 percent split is $50 to you before fees. Ten items a month at that level is $500 — real money, but it is ten full listing-and-shipping jobs, not passive income.

What Sells Best

Stick to items worth your time. A $15 item that takes 30 minutes to list, photograph, and ship earns you about $7.50 — a bad deal. Good concierge items are designer clothes and handbags, collectibles, working electronics, brand-name sneakers, and quality furniture that sells locally. Before you accept anything, check sold listings on eBay or Poshmark to confirm the price, and be honest with the client when an item is worth less than they think.

Getting Your First Client

Your first three clients are almost always people you know: a neighbor clearing a closet, a relative downsizing, a coworker moving house. Offer to sell five items as a trial, with a simple written agreement that states your commission, who pays shipping and platform fees, and when the owner gets paid — most experienced consignment sellers pay out 14 to 30 days after delivery. One good trial turns into referrals, because everyone knows someone with a full garage.

Set up a simple tracking system from day one. Give every item a code — client initials, item number, date — so nothing gets mixed up, and photograph items the moment they arrive. The biggest rookie mistake is taking in a pile of unlabeled stuff and then guessing whose it was.

The AI Add-On That Doubles the Service

The same clients who need a resale concierge often run a small business that misses calls after hours. Setting up one simple AI workflow for one type of business — for example, an AI receptionist that answers a dentist's after-hours calls and books appointments — is the natural second service. AI receptionist plans start around $29 a month for light use and run to $300 to $500 for busy lines (prices checked November 2026), and a basic setup takes an afternoon. You charge a setup fee plus a small monthly retainer to keep it running. It is a different skill, so learn it on one business type first — do not pitch it to every shop on the street.

The Honest Catch

This hustle has real friction. Items that do not sell still take up space in your home. Returns happen, and you need a written rule for who absorbs them. Some items need permission to sell — estate pieces, branded goods with authenticity questions — and you should never list something you cannot verify. And if you use a platform like Poshmark or eBay, their fees come out of the sale before anyone gets paid, so quote your client the net number, not the headline price.

A resale concierge is one of the few side hustles with a true zero-dollar start and a same-month first payment. The ceiling is your time: each item needs your hands. That is also why it works — most people will gladly pay someone else to do the work they keep putting off.

FAQ

Q: How much can you make as a resale concierge?
A: At a 40 to 50 percent commission, ten $100 sales a month is about $400 to $500 before platform fees and shipping. High-value items pay much more per job, but low-value items can eat your time for a few dollars each — so the real skill is choosing which items to accept.

Q: Do you need a license to sell other people's stuff?
A: Rules vary by state and city, and some places require a resale or secondhand dealer license, especially for jewelry or high-value goods. Always use a written consignment agreement that states your commission, fees, payout timing, and what happens to unsold items — never take an item without one.

Q: What is the fastest way to get your first resale concierge client?
A: Offer a five-item trial to someone you know who is clearing a closet or moving. Do the whole job well, pay them on time, and ask for a referral. One happy client with a full garage is worth more than a hundred cold messages.
