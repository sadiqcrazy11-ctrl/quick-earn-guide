Airbnb Host Fee Explained: What Hosts Pay in 2026

By Sadiq Khan · Published November 28, 2026 · 5 min read · Updated on November 28, 2026

Share:WhatsAppTelegramX

Quick Answer: Most Airbnb hosts now pay a single 15.5% service fee on the booking subtotal — cleaning fees included, taxes excluded — and guests see no separate fee. The old split fee (3% host + 14.1–16.5% guest) is being phased out and hosts cannot switch back.

If you learned Airbnb’s fees a year ago, what you learned is out of date. The famous 3% host fee — the number every host guide quoted for a decade — is being phased out. In its place is a single service fee that most hosts now pay: 15.5% of the booking subtotal, taken entirely from the host’s payout. The guest sees no separate fee at all. Here is how it works, what it costs, and what to do about it.

The single fee: 15.5% for most hosts

Under the single fee structure, most hosts pay 15.5% of the booking subtotal. The remaining hosts typically pay between 14% and 16%, and listings in Brazil and Mexico pay 16%. The fee base is the nightly price plus any fees the host adds — cleaning fee, pet fee, extra-guest fee — and taxes are excluded. The whole fee is deducted from the host’s payout, so the price the host sets is the price the guest pays, before taxes. Airbnb’s own example makes it concrete: set a price of $115, the guest sees $115, and the host earns $97 after the single fee. The fee is mandatory for most hosts, including hotels, serviced apartments, hosts using property management software, and hosts in countries covered by the structure.

The old split fee

Under the outgoing split fee, the host paid 3% — 4% for listings in Brazil and Mexico — and the guest paid a service fee of 14.1% to 16.5% of the booking subtotal, varying by booking and reaching the top end on cross-currency bookings. Airbnb is in the process of transitioning all home hosts to the single fee, and once a host is switched they cannot switch back. The single fee applies only to new bookings; bookings made before the switch keep the old fee terms. Migration deadlines passed in 2026 — September 15 for hosts outside the EEA, October 13 for hosts in the EEA and Switzerland — so by late 2026 the migration is essentially complete.

Worked example: a $550 booking

Take three nights at $150 plus a $100 cleaning fee: a $550 subtotal. Under the single fee at 15.5%, Airbnb takes $85.25 and the host keeps $464.75, while the guest pays $550 plus taxes. Under the old split fee, the host paid 3% — $16.50 — and kept $533.50, but the guest paid the $550 plus a 14.1–16.5% service fee of $77.55 to $90.75, for a total of $627.55 to $640.75 plus taxes. The host kept more under the split fee, and the guest paid more too. That is the trade: the single fee makes the listing look cheaper to the guest, and the host absorbs the difference.

What hosts should do

To keep the same payout after moving from the split fee to the single fee, a host must raise prices by about 14.8% — the ratio of 0.97 to 0.845. Before raising anything, check your own Earnings page: Airbnb does not tie a specific rate to a specific host type beyond Brazil and Mexico at 16%, so your own rate is the one that matters. Note also that the cleaning fee sits inside the fee base, so it is not fee-free. Separate fee types still exist elsewhere on the platform: service reservations typically carry a 15% fee with a $6 minimum, and experience reservations typically carry a 20% fee. And remember, GOD willing, that Airbnb reserves the right to change its service fees at any time — the rate you have today is not a promise.

FAQ

Q: What is the Airbnb host fee in 2026?
A: Most hosts pay a single service fee of 15.5% of the booking subtotal. Remaining hosts typically pay 14–16%, and listings in Brazil and Mexico pay 16%. The fee is deducted from the host’s payout; guests see no separate service fee.

Q: What happened to the 3% Airbnb host fee?
A: It is the legacy split fee, being phased out. Under it the host paid 3% (4% in Brazil and Mexico) and the guest paid 14.1–16.5%. Airbnb is transitioning all home hosts to the single fee, and once switched a host cannot switch back.

Q: Is the Airbnb service fee charged on the cleaning fee?
A: Yes. The fee base is the nightly price plus any fees the host adds — cleaning fee, pet fee, extra-guest fee. Taxes are excluded.

Q: Do guests pay a service fee on Airbnb?
A: Not under the single fee structure — the guest pays the price the host sets, before taxes, with no separate Airbnb service fee. Under the old split fee, guests paid 14.1–16.5% on top.

Q: What is the Airbnb fee for experiences?
A: Experience reservations typically carry a 20% service fee. Service reservations typically carry a 15% fee with a $6 minimum.
