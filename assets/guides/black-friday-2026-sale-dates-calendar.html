Black Friday 2026 Sale Dates Calendar: When Walmart, Target, and Best Buy Deals Actually Drop

By Sadiq Khan · Published October 31, 2026 · 7 min read

Share:WhatsAppTelegramX

Quick Answer: Black Friday 2026 is Friday, November 27, with Cyber Monday on November 30 — but sales have already started. Best Buy's DoorBOOsters began October 30 and Target's Deal of the Day runs daily from November 1. The deepest discounts usually land November 23–30, so buy early only at your target price.

Black Friday is not a day anymore. In 2026 it is a five-week season that quietly started in late October, and by the time the actual Friday arrives, some of the best deals are already gone while others have not even dropped yet. This calendar is the shopper's side of the season — when Walmart, Target, Best Buy, Lowe's, Costco, and Home Depot actually run their events, and how to decide whether to buy now or hold out. (If you sell online rather than shop, see our seller prep guide linked at the bottom — this page is for buyers.)

The season is big. The National Retail Federation forecasts November–December 2026 holiday sales of $1.013 trillion, up 4.2% on 2025. Retailers compete for that money by stretching their sales earlier every year — which is exactly why a calendar matters more than any single doorbuster ad.

The Black Friday 2026 Retailer Calendar

Dates below are the announced 2026 event windows. Retailers occasionally shift a start date or extend a sale, so treat this as your planning map and confirm the live dates on each store's site before a big purchase.

  • October 29 — Lowe's holiday deals begin. The earliest major start of the season. Lowe's also runs its 12 Days of Deals from November 16, with a fresh category on offer each day.
  • October 30 — Best Buy DoorBOOsters begin. Best Buy's early doorbuster-style drops open the electronics season a full month before Black Friday itself.
  • October 30 – November 30 — Costco's holiday savings run. A month-long warehouse event rather than a single weekend — worth checking early for big-ticket items, since warehouse stock does not get restocked mid-sale.
  • November 1 – December 24 — Target Deal of the Day. A new featured deal every day for nearly eight weeks. Target is also stocking 8,000+ new gifts this season, most priced at $20 or less — the budget-gift end of the market is Target's fight to win.
  • November 4 — Home Depot's Black Friday event begins. Tools, appliances, and home improvement deals start here, well before the main weekend.
  • November 5–7 — Target Early Black Friday. A three-day early event ahead of Target's main Black Friday push — a good window for toys and home goods before the crowds.
  • November 6 — Best Buy Early Black Friday begins. Best Buy rolls out early deals and adds new ones every Friday through the season, so a sold-out deal is not necessarily gone for good.
  • November 12–15 — Walmart Early Black Friday. Walmart's first big wave. Walmart+ members get 24 hours of early access to selected online drops — if something on your list is a known fast seller, that head start is the real value of the membership.
  • November 19 — Best Buy's main Black Friday event begins. A 10-day run covering the deepest electronics discounts of Best Buy's season.
  • November 23–29 — Walmart Black Friday Deals. Walmart's main event week. Online deals run all week; in stores, the Black Friday sale starts Friday, November 27 from 6 a.m. local time.
  • November 27 — Black Friday. The day itself. Still the single best day for TVs and the biggest headline doorbusters — but no longer the only day that matters.
  • November 28 — Small Business Saturday. Local and independent shops run their best offers of the year. Prices are rarely the absolute lowest, but this is where personalized and handmade gifts come from.
  • November 30 – December 2 — Walmart Cyber Monday. Online only, running three days. This — not Black Friday — is traditionally the better window for laptops and online-only bundles.
  • December 1–6 — Best Buy Cyber Week. Best Buy's online-focused follow-up week, often matching its Black Friday prices on items that did not sell through.
  • December 18–24 — Best Buy Last-Minute Savings. The final window: gift cards, accessories, and whatever stock is left, priced to move before Christmas.

When to Buy and When to Wait

The calendar only helps if you decide your number before the sales start. Set a target price for each item on your list — the price at which you would buy without regret — while prices are calm. Then the rule is simple: buy early only when a deal hits your target price. An early deal at your number is a win, not a compromise. An early deal above your number is just a sale banner doing its job.

If an item has not hit your target price, waiting is usually right. The deepest cuts of the season historically land November 23–30, and forecasters expect electronics discounts near 30% off list prices in that window. Category patterns are fairly consistent year to year: TVs are cheapest around Black Friday, while laptops tend to peak on Cyber Monday, when the online-only deals drop.

Two honest cautions. First, forecasts are not promises — a "near 30% off" prediction describes the best deals in a category, not every item in it, and popular models sell out rather than get cheaper. Second, know your safety net before you buy early: Best Buy, for example, runs a Holiday Price Match Guarantee, so if its own price drops later in the season you can ask for the difference. Policies like that turn an early buy from a gamble into a reasonable decision — check the current terms of whichever store you use, because the windows and exclusions change.

A Simple Shopping Plan

  1. This week: write the list, set the budget, and record today's regular prices for everything on it. You cannot judge a "deal" in November if you never knew the October price.
  2. November 1–7: watch Target's Deal of the Day and the November 5–7 Early Black Friday event for gifts and home items — the $20-and-under range is where Target is strongest.
  3. November 12–15: check Walmart's Early Black Friday for your list items. Walmart+ members should use the 24-hour early access on anything likely to sell out.
  4. November 23–30: this is the big-ticket window — TVs, appliances, and the items still sitting above your target price. Walmart's main week and Best Buy's event overlap here, so compare the two before paying.
  5. Cyber Monday (November 30): laptops and online-only deals. If a laptop is on your list, this is its day; Walmart's online event runs through December 2.

The Honest Part

No calendar — including this one — can guarantee you the lowest price on anything. Retailers move dates, prices differ by model and region, and a "sale" price is sometimes just the regular price wearing a red banner, which is why recording prices before the season matters more than any event date. What a calendar does give you is timing: you stop buying in a panic on whichever day you happen to see an ad, and start buying on the days each store is actually trying hardest. That, plus a target price you set in advance, is the whole strategy. It will not win you every item at its all-time low — it will stop you overpaying on most of them.

FAQ

Q: When is Black Friday 2026?
A: Friday, November 27, 2026, with Cyber Monday on November 30. But the season starts much earlier — Lowe's began October 29 and Best Buy's DoorBOOsters on October 30 — so waiting for the day itself means missing the early waves entirely.

Q: Should I buy early or wait for Black Friday?
A: Buy early only when a deal reaches the target price you set before the season. Otherwise wait: November 23–30 is historically the strongest window, with the deepest cuts on electronics. Where a store offers a holiday price-match guarantee, an early buy at your target price carries little risk.

Q: Which store starts its Black Friday sales first?
A: Lowe's, from October 29, followed by Best Buy's DoorBOOsters on October 30 and Target's Deal of the Day from November 1. Walmart's first big event is November 12–15, and Best Buy's main Black Friday event starts November 19.
