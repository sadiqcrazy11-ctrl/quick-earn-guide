How to Start Earning on YouTube

By Sadiq Khan · Published October 2026 · 3 min read

Share:WhatsAppTelegramX

YouTube is one of the most proven ways to earn online — and one of the slowest to start. Both things are true. The creators who make real money treated it like a part-time job for months before the first check arrived. Here is how it actually works.

The requirements: YouTube Partner Program

You cannot earn ad revenue until YouTube accepts you into the Partner Program. The current thresholds are:

  • Path A (long videos): 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 valid public watch hours in the last 12 months.
  • Path B (Shorts): 1,000 subscribers and 10 million valid Shorts views in the last 90 days.
  • You also need an AdSense account, two-step verification on your Google account, and no active Community Guidelines strikes.

Upcoming change for new applicants: From February 1, 2027, new applicants to the YouTube Partner Program ad and Premium revenue-sharing program will need 1,000 subscribers plus either 8,000 qualified public watch hours in the previous 365 days or 20 million qualified Shorts views in the previous 90 days — up from 4,000 watch hours and 10 million Shorts views. The 1,000-subscriber requirement is unchanged. Creators already in the program are not removed solely for falling below the new entry thresholds. Source: YouTube Help’s “Changes to the YouTube Partner Program” page; see also YouTube’s official Partner Earnings Overview.

Realistic timeline and pay

There is no reliable universal timeline for reaching monetization. Some channels qualify within months, others take years, and some never qualify. Progress depends on your topic, publishing consistency, audience response, watch time, and meeting YouTube’s monetization policies. Treat any timeline — including any planning scenario in this guide — as a planning scenario, not a prediction. Once monetized, the two formats are paid under different rules, and what a channel earns per 1,000 views depends heavily on your topic.

For eligible creators who accept the relevant monetization terms, YouTube pays 55% of net revenue from ads shown on public videos on the Watch Page. Shorts Feed monetization works differently: creators receive 45% of the revenue allocated to them from the Shorts Creator Pool. These percentages are not a guarantee of earnings, and actual income varies by format, audience, advertiser demand, and other factors. Check YouTube's official monetization documentation for current terms.

Official source: YouTube Partner Earnings Overview

What RPM means: RPM is creator earnings per 1,000 views — the amount the creator actually receives. It is not CPM, which is what advertisers pay before YouTube’s share. The figures in the table below are illustrative planning estimates only: they are not verified industry averages and they are not guaranteed payouts.

NicheIllustrative RPMWhy
Entertainment / vlogs$1–$4Advertisers pay less for casual viewing
Tech, education, how-to$4–$10Viewers are closer to buying decisions
Finance, business, software$10–$25+High-value advertisers compete for this audience

These figures are illustrative planning estimates, not verified industry averages or guaranteed payouts. Actual RPM varies by audience location, video format, monetized views, seasonality, and advertiser demand. Once you are monetized, replace these estimates with your own YouTube Analytics data. Estimated revenue = monetized views ÷ 1,000 × assumed RPM. Actual results may differ.

Your first steps

  1. Pick one narrow topic you can make 50 videos about — a specific hobby, skill, or problem you understand.
  2. Study 5 channels in that niche. Note which of their videos got far more views than their subscriber count. Make your own version of those ideas — never copies.
  3. Post consistently: one long video a week, or 3–5 Shorts a week if you go the Shorts route. Consistency beats expensive gear.
  4. Use what you own. A modern phone, free editing software (CapCut, DaVinci Resolve), and natural light are enough to start.
  5. Review your analytics every month. Double down on the topics and titles viewers actually click and watch.
  6. Apply to the Partner Program the moment you cross a threshold, and set up AdSense correctly with your legal name and US tax information.

Honest bottom line: YouTube rewards patience. If you need money this month, start with freelancing below and build your channel on the side. If you can give it a year, a channel becomes an asset that keeps earning from old videos.
