Find Clients With One Google Search (The LinkedIn Formula)

By Sadiq Khan · Published October 2026 · 4 min read

Share:WhatsAppTelegramX

Quick Answer: LinkedIn hides most profiles behind its own search limits and paid plans — but Google has already indexed millions of public LinkedIn profiles. One search line pulls up decision-makers by exact job title and city: the people who can actually hire a freelancer or a small agency. It costs nothing, needs no Sales Navigator, and takes about ten seconds.

Freelancers and small agencies use this to build client lists without paying for lead databases. It works because of one simple fact: a public LinkedIn profile is just a web page, and finding web pages is Google's whole job. You are not hacking anything — you are asking Google a sharper question.

The formula, part by part

site:linkedin.com/in "vp of sales" "austin, texas"
  • site:linkedin.com/in — tells Google: only individual LinkedIn profile pages. Not articles, not company pages, not job posts.
  • "vp of sales" — the exact job title, in quotes. Quotes force an exact match, so you get sales leaders instead of everyone who ever typed the word sales.
  • "austin, texas" — the city, also in quotes. Swap in any city where your clients are. Title + city is the whole trick: the right person, in the right place.

Copy a line, change two things

Every line below uses the same pattern. Keep the site: part exactly as it is, and swap the title and the city for your own market.

From search results to a client list

  1. Pick your buyer first. One service, one title, one city. “Video editing for real estate brokers in Miami” beats “anyone, anywhere” every time.
  2. Open profiles one at a time. Note each person's name, title, company, and profile link in a simple spreadsheet. Five columns is plenty.
  3. Find one personal detail per person. A recent post, a new location, a product launch — something their business is doing right now. That detail is what separates your message from spam.
  4. Build the list by hand — 20 to 30 profiles a day. Slow is not a flaw here. A short list of the right people beats a scraped list of thousands, and a human pace keeps your account safe (see the warning below).
  5. Send a short, personal message. Use the template below. If there's no reply, one follow-up after 4–5 days — then move on. No third chase, ever.

A first message that gets replies

Short, specific, and about them — not a pitch deck. Fill in the brackets from the personal detail you found in step 3:

Hi [Name] — noticed [Company] has been [specific detail: posting a lot of short videos lately / just opened a second location]. I help [type of business] with [your service + the result, e.g. turning those posts into enquiries]. I have two quick ideas for your page — want me to send them over? No charge, just the ideas.
Keep it manual — this part matters: Build the list by hand and write each message yourself. LinkedIn restricts accounts that view profiles at bot speed or use scraping and auto-messaging tools, and a restricted account ends your outreach completely. Never buy scraped lead lists, never blast the same copy to hundreds of people, and stop messaging anyone who says no. Twenty to thirty careful profiles a day is a pace you can keep up for months.

Honest bottom line: The formula finds people, not clients. Clients come from the personal detail, a relevant offer, and showing up every day. Expect a reply from roughly 1 in 10 good messages, and a first client after about 2–4 weeks of steady outreach. That is normal — and it is still faster than waiting for a platform algorithm to notice you.
