About this tool

What this debt payoff calculator does

There are two respected ways to attack multiple debts. The snowball pays the smallest balance first for fast wins that keep you going. The avalanche pays the highest interest rate first, which costs less in total interest. The difference is usually smaller than people expect — and the method you will actually stick with beats the mathematically perfect one you abandon. This calculator simulates both month by month on your real debts, with your real extra payment, and shows months to debt-free, total interest, and the payoff order for each.

A quick example

Run the defaults: $1,200 at 24%, $3,500 at 19%, and $800 at 15%, with $100 extra a month. Snowball clears the $800 card first, then the $1,200 card, then the $3,500 card. Avalanche clears the 24% card first. Both finish in roughly the same window, but avalanche saves a modest amount of interest — the exact figures are on the calculator, because they depend entirely on your balances and rates.

How to use it — 3 steps

  1. Enter each debt's balance, APR, and minimum payment. Leave unused rows at $0.
  2. Enter the extra amount you can pay every month on top of all minimums — even $25 changes the timeline.
  3. Compare the two timelines and interest totals, then commit to one order and automate it.

Who it is for

Anyone carrying two or more card, loan, or store debts and deciding where the next spare dollar should go.

Honest limitations

APRs and minimums move in real life, fees and new charges are excluded, and promotional 0% periods are not modeled — a 0% balance transfer can beat both methods if the transfer fee is low and the balance is cleared before the promo ends. If minimum payments do not cover interest, no strategy works until payments rise. Estimates only — not financial advice.

FAQ

Debt Payoff Calculator — common questions

Snowball or avalanche — which is better?

Avalanche always costs less interest, because it kills the most expensive debt first. Snowball wins on motivation: clearing a small debt fast keeps people going. The difference in total interest is often modest — pick the one you will actually follow for months.

What if my minimum payment does not cover the interest?

Then the balance grows every month no matter what strategy you pick. You need a larger payment, a lower rate (a balance-transfer offer or a hardship program), or help from a nonprofit credit counselor — the calculator flags this case.

Should I pay extra on one debt or spread it across all of them?

Concentrate it. Pay minimums on everything, then throw the entire extra payment at one target debt. Spreading the extra across debts keeps all of them alive longer and costs more interest.

Does paying off debt faster help my credit score?

Usually, yes — lower balances reduce utilization, which is a large part of most credit scores. But the main prize is the interest you stop paying, not the score.
