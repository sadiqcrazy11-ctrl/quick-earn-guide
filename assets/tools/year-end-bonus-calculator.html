About this tool

What this year-end bonus calculator does

A year-end bonus calculator explains the gap between the bonus your employer announces and the deposit that reaches your bank. Bonuses are supplemental wages under IRS rules, so employers withhold federal tax at a flat 22% — not at your regular paycheck rate — plus Social Security, Medicare, and state tax. On a $5,000 bonus that withholding is nearly 30% before state tax, which is why the take-home number always surprises people.

This calculator applies the flat supplemental method from IRS Publication 15: 22% federal withholding on supplemental wages up to $1 million per employer per year, 37% on the excess, Social Security at 6.2% only on wages under the $184,500 wage base, Medicare at 1.45% on everything, and the 0.9% Additional Medicare tax on wages over $200,000.

A quick example

Run the defaults: a $5,000 bonus with $60,000 of year-to-date wages and no state tax. Federal withholding is $5,000 × 22% = $1,100.00, Social Security is $310.00, Medicare is $72.50, and nothing is owed in Additional Medicare — take-home is $3,517.50, an effective withholding rate of 29.65%. With $180,000 of year-to-date wages, only $4,500 of a $10,000 bonus is under the wage base, so Social Security drops to $279.00 and take-home is $7,376.00. On a $1,200,000 bonus, the $200,000 above $1 million is withheld at 37%, and take-home is $868,161.00.

How to use it

  1. Enter the gross bonus from your bonus letter or pay stub.
  2. Enter year-to-date wages before the bonus — Social Security stops once wages pass $184,500.
  3. Add your state withholding percent if your state taxes bonuses, then read the take-home amount.

Who it is for

Employees receiving a year-end, holiday, or performance bonus, and anyone comparing a bonus offer against a salary raise.

Honest limitations

Your employer may use the aggregate method instead of the flat 22% method — combining the bonus with a regular paycheck and running it through the normal withholding tables — which can withhold more or less. This calculator also ignores 401(k) contributions taken from the bonus, garnishments, and local taxes. Most importantly, withholding is not your final tax: your actual tax is settled when you file, and a bonus can push you into a higher bracket or trigger a refund.

FAQ

Year-End Bonus Calculator — common questions

Why is my bonus taxed at 22% instead of my normal rate?

The IRS treats bonuses as supplemental wages. Under Publication 15, Section 7, employers may withhold a flat 22% federal tax on supplemental wages up to $1 million per year, instead of using your W-4 tables. It is a withholding shortcut, not a special bonus tax.

Is the 22% withholding my final tax on the bonus?

No. Withholding is a prepayment. Your actual tax on the bonus depends on your total income for the year and your marginal bracket when you file. If your bracket is higher than 22%, you may owe more; if lower, you may get some back as a refund.

Does Social Security tax apply to bonuses?

Yes, at 6.2%, but only on wages up to the annual wage base — $184,500 in 2026. If your year-to-date wages already passed the wage base, no Social Security is withheld from the bonus at all. Medicare at 1.45% applies to the whole bonus regardless.

What happens if my bonus pushes supplemental wages over $1 million?

The portion above $1 million in supplemental wages from one employer in the calendar year is withheld at a mandatory 37%. The first $1 million stays at 22%. The threshold is cumulative for the year, not per bonus.
