About this tool

What this W-2 vs 1099 calculator does

A salary and a contract rate look comparable until you count what sits behind them. The W-2 side includes employer-paid health insurance, a retirement match, and paid days off — and the employer pays half of your Social Security and Medicare taxes. The 1099 side pays a bigger headline number, then hands you both halves of those taxes, your own insurance, your own retirement, and every unpaid hour of admin, quoting, and chasing invoices. This W-2 vs 1099 calculator puts real 2026 federal tax math on both sides and finds the contract rate where the two actually break even.

A quick example

Take the defaults: a $70,000 salary with $6,000 of health cover, a $2,000 retirement match, and 15 paid days off, against $60 an hour for 30 billable hours a week over 48 weeks with $3,000 of expenses. After tax, the W-2 side nets about $66,075 and the 1099 side about $63,394 — the salary wins by roughly $2,681 a year, even though the contract rate looks far higher per hour. The breakeven 1099 rate is about $62.85 an hour: below it, the salary is the better deal on these numbers.

How to use it — 3 steps

  1. Enter the W-2 offer: salary, the dollar value of employer health insurance and retirement match, and paid days off.
  2. Enter the 1099 offer: hourly rate, honest billable hours, working weeks, and yearly business expenses.
  3. Compare the two net figures and the breakeven rate — then decide with the full picture, not the headline.

Who it is for

Employees weighing a jump to contracting, contractors comparing a staff offer, and freelancers deciding whether a "great rate" is actually great. It is also useful for negotiating: walking in with a breakeven number is stronger than walking in with a feeling.

Honest limitations

Money is not the whole comparison. The calculator cannot price job security, the quality of the health plan, career growth, flexibility, or the stress of finding the next contract — all real factors that cut both ways. It also assumes the 1099 hours are all billable and paid, which overstates contract income for most people. And it is federal-only: state tax and the QBI deduction can move both sides. FAQ

W-2 vs 1099 Calculator — common questions

How does the W-2 vs 1099 calculator work?

It totals each side's compensation, subtracts real 2026 federal taxes — employee FICA plus income tax on the W-2 side, self-employment tax plus income tax on the 1099 side — and compares what is left. Then it solves for the 1099 hourly rate at which both sides net the same amount. Estimates only — not financial or tax advice.

Why do I enter benefits as a dollar value?

Because health insurance and a retirement match are pay, even though they never hit your bank account. A $70,000 salary with $8,000 of benefits is $78,000 of compensation. Ignoring benefits is the most common way people overvalue a contract rate.

What is the breakeven 1099 rate?

The hourly contract rate at which the 1099 side nets exactly what the W-2 side nets, after tax, on the hours and expenses you entered. Above it, contracting pays more on these numbers; below it, the salary does. It is a negotiating floor, not a target — aim above it.

What does this comparison leave out?

State tax, the QBI deduction, tax credits, unpaid admin time on the 1099 side, and everything money cannot measure: security, flexibility, benefits quality, and career growth. It also assumes the contract hours are steady all year, which is rarely true. Use it as the financial half of the decision, not the whole decision.
