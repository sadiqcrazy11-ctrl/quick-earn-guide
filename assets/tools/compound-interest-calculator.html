About this tool

What this compound interest calculator does

Compound interest means interest earns interest: each period's growth joins the balance and earns in the next period. This compound interest calculator applies that math with a starting amount, monthly contributions, a rate you choose, and a compounding frequency from annual to daily — and shows a year-by-year table so you can watch the interest column catch up with, and then pass, the contributions column. That crossover is the whole argument for starting early.

A quick example

With the defaults — $5,000 to start, $200 a month, 7% compounded monthly over 10 years — you contribute $29,000 of your own money and the future value is about $44,665, of which about $15,665 is interest. The same $5,000 at 10% compounded annually for 3 years with no contributions grows to exactly $13,310. And $1,000 plus $200 a month at 7% monthly for 10 years lands at about $36,627, matching the investment calculator's projection — the same math, with the year-by-year table and frequency choice added here.

How to use it — 3 steps

  1. Enter a starting amount (0 is fine), a monthly contribution you can keep up, and an annual rate.
  2. Pick the years and the compounding frequency — monthly matches most savings accounts and funds.
  3. Read the future value, the contributions-versus-interest split, the effective annual rate, and the yearly table.

Who it is for

Savers comparing accounts with different compounding frequencies, beginners who want to see compounding year by year rather than as one final number, and anyone testing whether a higher rate or a longer horizon helps a goal more. For a retirement-focused projection with salary and employer match, use the 401(k) calculator.

Honest limitations

The rate is assumed constant, which no real investment delivers. Taxes on interest, account fees and inflation all reduce the real outcome. Contributions are spread evenly across compounding periods, which slightly differs from depositing once a month when compounding is annual or daily. The result is an illustrative projection, not a forecast and not financial advice.

FAQ

Compound Interest Calculator — common questions

How does the compound interest calculator work?

It divides each year into compounding periods. Every period, the balance grows by the annual rate divided by the number of periods, and a share of the monthly contributions is added. The year-by-year table records the balance, total contributions and total interest at each year end, so the compounding curve is visible instead of hidden inside one final number.

What is the difference between the interest rate and the effective annual rate?

The rate you enter is the nominal annual rate. The effective annual rate is what compounding actually delivers over a year: one plus the rate divided by the number of periods, raised to the number of periods, minus one. More frequent compounding raises the effective rate slightly — 7% compounded daily behaves like about 7.25% compounded annually.

Does compounding frequency matter much?

Less than the rate and the years do. Moving from annual to daily compounding on the same rate adds a small boost. Moving from 5% to 7%, or from 10 years to 20, changes the outcome far more. Frequency is worth checking when comparing two accounts at the same headline rate; it is not worth chasing on its own.

How is this different from the investment calculator?

The investment calculator projects monthly compounding only, with a single final figure. This calculator adds the compounding-frequency choice, the effective annual rate, the simple-interest comparison, and the full year-by-year table showing contributions versus interest at every year end.
