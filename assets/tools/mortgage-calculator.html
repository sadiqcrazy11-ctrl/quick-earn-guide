About this tool

What this mortgage calculator does

A mortgage calculator that only shows principal and interest understates the real bill. Your lender collects property tax and insurance with the payment, PMI applies when the down payment is under 20%, and HOA dues come on top. This mortgage calculator adds all of it into one monthly PITI figure, shows the total interest over the life of the loan, and shows how little of the balance disappears in the first five years — because early payments are mostly interest.

A quick example

With the defaults — a $400,000 home, $80,000 down, 6.5% over 30 years — the loan is $320,000 and principal and interest run $2,022.62 a month. Add $400 of property tax and $150 of insurance and the full PITI payment is $2,572.62. Over the life of the loan you would pay about $408,142 in interest, more than the amount borrowed. That is the honest cost of a 30-year term, and the reason a 15-year term or extra principal payments change the picture so much.

How to use it — 3 steps

  1. Enter the home price, your down payment, the rate you were quoted, and the term.
  2. Add yearly property tax and insurance, PMI if your down payment is under 20%, and any monthly HOA dues.
  3. Read the PITI total, the lifetime interest, and the five-year balance — then change one input at a time to see what moves the payment most.

Who it is for

First-time buyers checking whether a listing is affordable, and anyone refinancing who wants to compare the old payment against the new one with the same honest inputs on both sides.

Honest limitations

Property tax and insurance change over time; this calculator holds your yearly figures flat. PMI is modeled as a flat percentage and does not drop off at 20% equity the way real PMI does. Adjustable rates, points, closing costs and buydowns are not modeled. The result is a planning estimate, not a lender quote and not financial advice.

FAQ

Mortgage Calculator — common questions

How does the mortgage calculator work?

It divides the loan into equal monthly payments using the standard amortization formula: payment equals loan times the monthly rate, divided by one minus the monthly rate factor over the full term. Property tax and insurance are divided by 12, PMI is applied as a yearly percentage of the loan divided by 12, and HOA is added as entered. The PITI total is the sum of all of them.

What is PITI?

PITI stands for principal, interest, taxes and insurance: the four parts of the monthly check most homeowners write. Principal and interest go to the lender, taxes and insurance usually go into an escrow account the lender pays from. HOA dues are sometimes added as a fifth part, which is why this calculator shows PITI plus HOA as the headline total.

When do I need PMI?

Private mortgage insurance is usually required on a conventional loan when the down payment is under 20% of the home price. It protects the lender, not you, and it typically ends once you reach 20% equity through payments, appreciation, or a new appraisal. FHA loans use a different mortgage insurance premium with its own rules.

Why is the balance barely lower after 5 years?

Because amortization is front-loaded with interest. On a 30-year loan the early payments are mostly interest and only a small slice pays down principal. The principal portion grows every month, which is why extra principal payments early in the loan save far more interest than the same extra payments near the end.
