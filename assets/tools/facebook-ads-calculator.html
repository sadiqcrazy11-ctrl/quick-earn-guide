About this tool

What this Facebook Ads calculator does

Meta does not sell Facebook ads at a fixed price. Every impression and click is priced by auction, so the only honest way to judge a campaign is to measure what you actually paid: CPM is spend divided by impressions times 1,000, CPC is spend divided by clicks, CTR is clicks divided by impressions, and ROAS is revenue divided by spend. This calculator runs all five from your numbers.

A quick example

$500 of spend, 50,000 impressions, 900 clicks, 40 conversions and $940 of revenue: CPM $10.00, CPC $0.56, CTR 1.80%, cost per conversion $12.50, ROAS 1.88x — right on the 2026 ecommerce median.

How to use it — 3 steps

  1. Enter your ad spend and the impressions, clicks and conversions it bought.

FAQ

Facebook Ads Calculator — common questions

Does Facebook charge a platform fee for ads?

No. Meta charges per impression or click through its auction — there is no fixed platform fee or subscription. What you pay depends on competition for your audience, your objective and the season.

What is a good CPC on Facebook Ads in 2026?

WordStream’s 2026 US benchmarks put median CPC at $0.60 for traffic campaigns and $1.80 for lead campaigns. Finance and legal niches pay far more; apparel and ecommerce pay less.

What is a good ROAS on Facebook Ads?

Triple Whale’s 2026 ecommerce median is 1.88x — $1.88 back per $1 spent. But “good” means above your own break-even ROAS, which depends on your margin: at 40% margin you need 2.5x just to break even.

What is the difference between CPM, CPC and CTR?

CPM is what 1,000 impressions cost; CPC is what one click costs; CTR is the share of impressions that became clicks. CPM measures reach cost, CPC measures traffic cost, CTR measures how well the creative earns the click.
