About this tool

What this 401k retirement calculator does

Three things build a 401(k): your contributions, your employer's match, and growth on both. This 401k retirement calculator projects all three year by year to your retirement age, using the match formula most plans actually use — the employer matches a percentage of what you contribute, up to a cap on your salary. It also checks each year's contribution against the 2026 IRS limit for your age that year, and caps the projection there with a warning, because plans stop deferrals at the limit.

A quick example

Take a 30-year-old with no balance, a $50,000 salary, a 10% contribution and a 50% match up to 6% of pay, growing at 7% with no salary growth. That is $5,000 a year from the worker and $1,500 a year from the employer. By 65 the projection reaches about $898,540 — $175,000 of worker contributions, $52,500 of match, and about $671,040 of growth. The match and the growth together are worth more than four times the worker's own money. That is why starting early beats starting big.

How to use it — 3 steps

  1. Enter your age, retirement age, current balance and salary.
  2. Set your contribution percentage and the employer match terms from your plan documents: the match rate and the salary cap it applies up to.
  3. Pick an expected return and salary growth rate, then read the projected balance, the three-way split, and the year-by-year table.

Who it is for

Employees deciding a contribution percentage, anyone who has never checked whether they capture the full match, and workers near 50 or 60 who want to see what catch-up contributions add. It covers 401(k), 403(b), most 457(b) and TSP plans, which share the same 2026 deferral limit.

Honest limitations

The projection assumes one smooth return every year, contributions at year end, and salary growth applied evenly. Real returns vary, sometimes sharply; plan fees, taxes on withdrawal, loans, hardship withdrawals and job changes are excluded; and the IRS limit is held at its 2026 value for every future year, when in reality it is adjusted for inflation. The result is a planning estimate, not a forecast and not financial advice.

FAQ

401k Retirement Calculator — common questions

How does the 401k retirement calculator work?

Each projected year, it grows the starting balance by the return you entered, then adds your contribution and the employer match for that year. Your contribution is your percentage of that year's salary, capped at the IRS limit for your age that year. Salary grows by your growth rate each year. The table shows every year so the compounding is visible, not hidden.

What is the 2026 401(k) contribution limit?

The employee elective-deferral limit for 2026 is $24,500, per IRS Notice 2025-67 and news release IR-2025-111. Workers age 50 and older can add an $8,000 catch-up for $32,500, and workers ages 60 to 63 can add an $11,250 super catch-up for $35,750, if the plan permits catch-ups. Employer match does not count against the $24,500 limit.

Does the employer match count toward my limit?

No. The $24,500 limit covers only money deferred from your own pay. Employer matching and profit-sharing dollars count toward a separate, much larger total limit on all annual additions, which is $72,000 for 2026. That is why capturing the full match never costs you deferral room.

What return should I assume?

Long-run stock market averages are often quoted around 7 to 10% a year before inflation, but any single decade can land far from that. Run the projection at a cautious return, such as 5%, and at an optimistic one, such as 8%, and plan around the cautious outcome. A projection is a sketch of what could happen, not a promise of what will.
