About this tool

What this 50-state paycheck calculator does

Most paycheck calculators ask you to type in a state tax percentage — which means guessing the one number the whole answer depends on. This one does not. It carries the real 2026 tax brackets for all 50 states plus Washington DC, from the Tax Foundation's 2026 table: graduated brackets where states have them, flat rates where they do not, each state's own standard deduction and exemptions, and the nine states with no wage income tax at all. It annualizes your paycheck, runs it through the 2026 federal brackets, applies FICA with the $184,500 Social Security wage base, and divides everything back into a per-paycheck figure.

A quick example

Run the defaults: $2,500 every two weeks ($65,000 a year), single, California, no pre-tax deductions. Federal withholding is $216.15 a paycheck, Social Security $155.00, Medicare $36.25, and California state tax $82.34 — at an 8% marginal rate and a 3.29% effective rate. Take-home is $2,010.26 a paycheck, $52,266.65 a year. Switch the state to Texas and the same paycheck nets $2,092.60 — $82.34 more every two weeks, $2,140.85 more a year, from the state line alone.

How to use it — 3 steps

  1. Enter your gross pay per paycheck and how often you are paid.
  2. Pick your filing status and your state — the state brackets fill in automatically.
  3. Add pre-tax deductions (401(k), health premiums) to see how they change the federal and state lines.

Who it is for

Anyone comparing job offers across states, remote workers choosing where to live, and new hires checking whether their first paycheck looks right.

Honest limitations

Local income taxes (New York City, many Ohio and Pennsylvania towns, and others) are not modeled, and neither are state credits, phase-outs, or the quirks listed in the disclaimer — Alabama's federal-tax deduction, Utah's taxpayer tax credit, and South Carolina's mid-year rate change. Your employer's withholding tables and your W-4 will make a real paycheck differ slightly. Estimates only — not tax advice.

FAQ

50-State Paycheck Calculator — common questions

How is state tax calculated here?

From the Tax Foundation's 2026 state brackets: your annual pay minus pre-tax deductions, minus the state's own standard deduction and personal exemptions, run through that state's brackets — graduated, flat, or zero. The marginal rate shown is the rate on your next dollar; the effective rate is the tax divided by your gross pay.

Which states have no income tax on wages?

Nine: Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington and Wyoming. Washington taxes capital gains, not wages, and New Hampshire taxes interest and dividends, not wages.

Why does my real paycheck differ from this estimate?

Your employer withholds using IRS Publication 15-T tables and your W-4, not this annual-estimate method — so small differences are normal. Bonuses, overtime, local tax, and benefits also move the real number.

What is not included in this calculator?

Local and city income taxes, state tax credits and phase-outs, Alabama's deduction for federal income tax paid, Utah's taxpayer tax credit, and South Carolina's top rate change mid-2026 (6.0% in the first half, scheduled to revert to 6.2% on July 1). Check your state revenue department's site before relying on any figure.
