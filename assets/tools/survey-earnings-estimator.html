About this tool

What this survey earnings estimator does

Survey sites advertise pay per survey. What they do not advertise is the screen-out: the 2–5 minute screener you answer for free, only to be told you do not fit the quota. Expect to be disqualified from roughly half of what you start, and the advertised rate collapses. This survey earnings estimator models that honestly two ways: it shows the tested hourly range for each major panel from our 2026 surveys guide, and it computes an effective hourly rate from your own pay, survey length, and screen-out rate — so you see the number your time actually earns.

A quick example

Take the defaults: 30 minutes a day, 5 days a week, a 50% screen-out rate, $1.50 per completed 15-minute survey, and 3 minutes lost per screen-out. Your effective rate is $5.00 an hour — about $12.50 a week and $54 a month. On Prolific's tested $8–$14 range, the same 2.5 hours a week would be $20–$35 a week. The gap between the two is why platform choice matters more than effort.

How to use it — 3 steps

  1. Pick a platform and set your real schedule — minutes per day and days per week you will actually do.
  2. Set your screen-out rate (50% is the honest default) and the average pay, length, and screener time from your own experience.
  3. Read your effective rate and the platform comparison table, and judge any panel by the monthly figure, not the per-survey headline.

Who it is for

Anyone considering paid surveys as a side income, and anyone already doing them who suspects the hourly rate is worse than the app suggests. It is also a useful check before you believe a screenshot of survey earnings: run the same numbers here and see what the screen-outs do to them.

Honest limitations

The platform ranges are tested averages, not promises: Prolific's volume depends on your demographics, and no panel can give you surveys that do not exist. The custom model assumes every attempt is either a full survey or a screener — real sessions have loading time, attention checks, and occasional rejections on top. A 90-day test across six apps averaged $8.19 an hour at 5 hours a week; treat anything far above that as marketing. Estimates only — not financial advice.

FAQ

Survey Earnings Estimator — common questions

How does the survey earnings estimator work?

Two ways. The platform ranges are tested 2026 figures from our surveys guide, already measured after screen-outs. The custom rate models your own numbers: expected pay per attempt is your survey pay times the share you qualify for, divided by the expected time per attempt — full survey length when you qualify, screener time when you do not. Estimates only — not financial advice.

Why is my effective rate so much lower than the pay per survey?

Because roughly half of attempts end in a screen-out that pays nothing but still costs 2–5 minutes. A $1.50 survey that takes 15 minutes looks like $6 an hour; with a 50% screen-out rate and 3-minute screeners, the real rate is $5 an hour — and with worse luck, far less.

Which survey platform pays the best?

Prolific, by a wide margin: $8–$14 an hour in tests, because it hosts research studies and enforces an $8/hour pay floor. The catch is a waitlist, an identity check, and study volume that depends on your demographics. Most consumer panels pay $1–$5 an hour after screen-outs.

Can online surveys replace a job?

No. Realistic totals run $45–$90 a month on average platforms at 30–45 minutes a day, or $70–$180 on good ones. A 90-day test at 5 hours a week earned $573.65 in total. Surveys are waiting-room money — useful, honest, and capped.
