About this tool

What this side hustle income calculator does

This side hustle income calculator answers the simplest question in the gig economy: what is this actually worth? Enter what you charge — per hour, or per job — and how much of it you do in a typical week. It projects your weekly income, your monthly income (worked out over an average month of about 4.33 weeks, since months are not exactly four weeks), and your yearly total, so a small weekly habit gets the big-picture context it deserves.

A quick example

The defaults tell a typical story: $25 an hour for 10 hours a week is $250 a week — easy to shrug at. Run it through the calculator and it becomes about $1,083 a month and $13,000 a year, which is a car, a serious emergency fund, or a real dent in a loan. That is why the yearly figure matters: weekly numbers undersell what a steady habit is actually worth.

How to use it — 3 steps

  1. Enter what you charge per hour — or your price per job, if that is how you get paid.
  2. Enter how many hours (or jobs) you realistically do per week, on an average week, not your best week.
  3. Read the three numbers: weekly, monthly, and yearly. The yearly figure is usually the one that changes minds.

Who it is for

Students and part-timers weighing whether a gig is worth their evenings, anyone comparing two hustles against each other, and people setting an income target — like an extra $500 a month — who want to work backwards to the hours and rate it actually requires.

Honest limitations

These are gross figures, before taxes and expenses, and they assume steady demand every single week. Real gigs have gaps, cancellations, slow seasons, and setup time that pays nothing. Treat the yearly number as a ceiling for a consistent year, not a salary. Estimates only — not financial advice.

FAQ

Side Hustle Income Calculator — common questions

How does the side hustle income calculator work?

Enter what you charge per hour (or per job) and how many hours (or jobs) you do each week. The calculator multiplies out to a projected monthly income (using an average month of about 4.33 weeks) and a yearly income. Estimates only — actual income depends on demand and how consistently you work.

Is the result before or after tax?

Before. The figures are gross income. Self-employment income is taxable, and many people set aside roughly a fifth to a third for taxes and expenses — your own situation varies, and this is not tax advice.

Should I enter an hourly rate or a price per job?

Whichever matches how you are actually paid. If you charge $40 to mow a lawn and do five lawns a week, enter 40 as the rate and 5 as the weekly units — the weekly, monthly, and yearly math works exactly the same.

How many hours a week should I start with?

Fewer than you think. Five to ten hours a week is sustainable next to a job or school, and the calculator shows even that adds up over a year. Burnout ends more side hustles than low rates do.
