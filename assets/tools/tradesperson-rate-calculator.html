About this tool

What this tradesperson rate calculator does

Most tradespeople set their hourly rate by asking around, copying a competitor, or picking a number that sounds fair. That is how you end up busy all year and broke at the end of it. In one widely shared Reddit thread, an electrician described charging about $140 an hour while needing roughly $311 an hour to actually cover his costs — working flat out, at a loss, without realizing it. This tradesperson rate calculator works the number out properly: your target income plus your real overhead, divided by the hours you can honestly bill.

A quick example

Take the defaults: an $80,000 target income, $28,800 a year of overhead across truck, tools, insurance, rent and health cover, 30 billable hours a week, and 48 working weeks. Your floor — the rate below which you lose money — is about $75.56 an hour. Add a 20% profit margin and the recommended rate is about $90.67 an hour. If you have been charging $60, you have not been cheap — you have been paying for the privilege of working.

How to use it — 3 steps

  1. Pick your trade, then replace every overhead default with your real annual costs. The defaults are starting points, not facts about your business.
  2. Set your honest billable hours — the hours clients actually pay for, not the hours you are awake — and your working weeks, allowing for holidays and slow spells.
  3. Read your floor rate and your recommended rate, edit the price book to your real jobs, and run every quote through the quote check before you send it.

Who it is for

Self-employed electricians, plumbers, HVAC techs and roofers pricing their own work, tradespeople thinking of going out on their own, and anyone who has ever finished a busy month wondering where the money went. If you employ a crew, run the numbers per billable tech instead of per business.

Honest limitations

The calculator knows nothing about your market: in a small town, the rate it gives may be more than local customers will pay, which is a real problem the math cannot solve — the honest answers are lower overhead, more billable hours, or a different market, not quietly working below your floor. It also treats your target income as pre-tax; income and self-employment taxes still come out of it, so check the side hustle tax calculator to see what you actually keep. FAQ

Tradesperson Rate Calculator — common questions

How does the tradesperson rate calculator work?

Enter your target annual income, your yearly overhead (truck, tools, insurance, rent, health cover), your billable hours per week and working weeks per year. It adds income and overhead, divides by your billable hours, and gives your break-even floor rate — then adds your profit margin for a recommended rate, and builds a price book from it. Estimates only — not financial advice.

Why is my break-even rate so much higher than my old hourly wage?

Because an employee's wage is only part of what the job costs: the employer also pays payroll taxes, insurance, the truck, the tools, and your paid time off. On your own, all of that comes out of your rate — and only part of your week is billable, since quoting, driving, and admin pay nothing.

What should I put for overhead if I am just starting out?

Start with the defaults, then replace each one with a real quote or bill as soon as you have it: your actual insurance premium, your real truck payment and fuel, your tool spending last year. Guessed overhead is the number one reason rate calculators mislead — a floor built on guesses is still a guess.

What happens if I quote below my break-even rate?

You pay the difference out of your own income. The quote check on this page flags it: enter any quote and its hours, and it shows the effective hourly rate against your floor. Occasionally taking a small loss for a strategic customer is a choice — doing it unknowingly, job after job, is how trades businesses fail quietly.
