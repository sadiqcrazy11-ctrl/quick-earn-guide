About this tool

What this raise calculator does

Two forces shrink every raise before it reaches your bank account. First, the new dollars are taxed at your marginal rates — federal income tax plus FICA — so a $3,250 gross raise might keep only about $2,500. Second, inflation shrinks what those dollars buy: a 5% raise in a 3% inflation year is roughly a 1.9% real raise. This calculator runs both checks with the verified 2026 federal brackets and standard deductions, and tells you plainly whether the raise grows your purchasing power or just keeps pace.

A quick example

Run the defaults: $65,000 salary, a 5% raise, single filer, 3% inflation. The gross raise is $3,250 (new salary $68,250). Extra federal tax plus FICA comes to roughly $750, leaving about $2,500 after tax — about $208 more a month. After 3% inflation, the real raise is about 1.9%: your purchasing power genuinely grows, just by much less than the headline 5%.

How to use it — 3 steps

  1. Enter your current salary and either the raise percentage or the exact new salary — the new-salary field wins if both are filled.
  2. Pick your filing status: it sets the standard deduction and bracket thresholds.
  3. Enter the inflation rate you expect (or the latest CPI figure) to see the real, purchasing-power raise.

Who it is for

Employees evaluating a raise or a job offer, anyone comparing two offers with different salaries, and managers who want to quote honest numbers.

Honest limitations

State and local taxes are excluded and can take a large additional slice. Pre-tax deductions (401k, HSA, health premiums) change taxable income and are not modeled. The inflation check uses one flat rate for the year. Estimates only — not tax advice.

FAQ

Raise Calculator — common questions

Why is my raise worth less than the gross amount?

The extra dollars are taxed at your marginal rates — federal income tax at your top bracket plus 7.65% FICA (6.2% Social Security + 1.45% Medicare). On a typical middle-income salary, roughly 20–30% of a raise goes to tax before it reaches you.

Does a raise push me into a higher tax bracket?

Only the dollars above the threshold pay the higher rate — brackets are marginal, so a raise never makes you poorer by crossing one. What it does do is tax the top slice of the raise at the higher rate.

What is a 'real' raise?

A raise after inflation: (1 + raise%) ÷ (1 + inflation%) − 1. A 5% raise with 3% inflation is about a 1.9% real raise. If inflation beats your raise percentage, your purchasing power fell even though your salary rose.

Are state taxes included?

No — this calculator is federal only. State income tax takes an additional slice that varies from 0% (in nine states) to over 10% in the highest-tax states. Add your state rate mentally for a closer figure.
